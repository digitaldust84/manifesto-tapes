## 1. Welcome post — "what is this"

*Suggested title: **Welcome to the evidence locker***

---

You've found the written companion to *The Manifesto Tapes* — a podcast about the oldest machine ever built, and how to walk away from it.

Here's the premise, stated plainly: every system you've ever lived under kept a ledger with your name in it. Four thousand five hundred years ago it was wet clay in the city of Ur — a scribe pressing a reed stylus into a tablet, recording how much barley a farmer owed the temple. Name. Debt. Amount. Today it's databases and models and feeds that know exactly what keeps your thumb moving at 2 AM. The substrate changed. The book didn't.

The show asks one question across six tapes: **who has been writing the ledger of your life — and what will it cost to take the pen back?**

Each tape follows one transformation of the ledger — clay, paper, database, model — from Sumerian mud to the silicon horizon. Through Gilgamesh's terror of being forgotten. Through Babel's scattered tongues. Through the rocket-fuel rituals of Pasadena and the underground cities built for the end of the world. Guiding you are two hosts: **Sable Vane**, an archivist of lost futures who started out not believing any of this, and **Cog**, a tinkerer's construct who asks the naive questions nobody else will. By the final tape, neither of them is who they were at the start.

This publication is where the receipts live. The tapes are the spell — atmosphere, dread, the story. Here is the evidence locker:

- **Transcripts** of every tape, so you can read what you heard.
- **Sources** — the scholarship, the archives, the sixteen-source deep end this whole thing was built from. When the show says there's a tomb under the Euphrates, here's where you see why.
- **The heist kit** — the Field Notes issues. Each one hands you a single practical tool: killing your SSO logins, running a model on your own machine, building the mesh. By the end of the season you'll be holding the complete kit.
- **Marginalia from the hosts** — Sable's dry footnotes, Cog's earnest questions. They don't stop talking just because the episode ended.

A few promises, stated up front. Every tape descends into dread and climbs back out before it ends — the way out is the point, not the dread. The voices you hear are synthetic; the research, writing, and direction are human. And nothing here will tell you what to think. The ledger taught you to be documented. This is about becoming illegible — on your own terms.

Tape One is *The Well and the Refusal*. It starts at 2:14 in the morning, with you, awake, scrolling.

The book is open. Let's see whose name is really in it.

### The evidence locker

The historical archives and scholarship behind Tape 1:

- **The Akkadian Period Archives (Sheep, Barley, and Rations):** Cuneiform tablets dating from the Akkadian period (2300 BC–2150 BC) document the administrative foundation of early civilization, containing records ranging from scholarly texts to exact tallies of sheep and barley rations. These artifacts capture the scribal training process for the state's archives during the rise of the very first recorded empire.
- **Girsu and Early Economic Spreadsheets:** Archaeological investigations at Girsu provide concrete insight into the Akkadian bureaucracy, detailing how state power was maintained through economic record-keeping, administrative spreadsheets, and the centralization of resources under figures like Naram-Sin.
- **The Material Evidence of Imperial Control:** Excavations of archival materials preserved in situ offer the earliest physical evidence of systemic imperial control, demonstrating how state-level administrative tracking was physically engineered into the ancient world.
