# The Manifesto Tapes — Production Plan
**For Ash · DRAFT · 2026-10-10 · Concept 4: AI-hosted spoken-word season**

The pitch: your *Sovereign manifesto* becomes a serialized audio piece — one AI voice, sound design, atmosphere. Less "podcast," more audio zine. Six tapes, one season, then you decide if there's a season two. Your voice is never required anywhere in this plan. Total startup cost: **$0**.

**Legend:** 📱 = phone/car-friendly step · 🖥️ = desk step

## The season at a glance

Six episodes, 8–12 minutes each, one idea per tape. The manifesto is the spine; each episode takes one passage and expands it with a thread from your notebook:

| # | Tape | Core idea | Notebook thread |
|---|------|-----------|-----------------|

**Episode shape** (every tape follows this arc):
1. **Cold open** (30 sec) — one striking line, no intro music yet. Hook first.
2. **The reading** (5–8 min) — the performed passage, verbatim where it sings.
3. **Coda** (1–2 min) — a short reflection bridging to the next tape. Ends on a question, not an answer.

## Phase 1 — Casting (the fun tinker) 📱

This is your first afternoon with the project, and it's pure play: find the voice that *sounds* like the Manifesto sounds in your head.

**Where to browse:**
- **ElevenLabs Voice Library** (elevenlabs.fm/voices — free to browse and test; generation uses your 10k free monthly credits) — filter by *Narration* and *Deep*. Hundreds of community voices.
- **Play.ht** (play.ht — free plan to test) — smaller library, fine as a second stop.

**How to run the casting call:**
1. 📱 Pick one paragraph from the manifesto — the one that gives you chills. This is your audition script; use the *same* paragraph for every voice.
2. 📱 Generate it in 4–5 candidate voices. Listen back-to-back, eyes closed, in the car.
3. 📱 Favorite your top 2. Sleep on it. Pick one the next day.

**What to listen for:**
- **Weight, not volume.** A manifesto is a declaration — it needs authority, not shouting. Unrushed beats intense.
- **Room to breathe.** Spoken word dies when the voice rushes. Favor voices with natural pauses.
- **Texture match.** Slight grit → steampunk lane. Clean and glassy → cyberpunk lane. Pick the lane that matches the cover art you'll make in Phase 4.
- **The stress test:** make it say *"Sumer," "densities," "Gilgamesh," "Enkidu."* Esoteric vocab is where AI voices embarrass themselves — if it mangles the words, it's out. (Fixable later with phonetic spelling, but a voice that nails them cold is a keeper.)
- **Consistency:** you'll live with this voice for six episodes. Favorite/save it the moment you choose.

**Red flags:** voices that sound like a GPS, voices that can't do quiet, any voice that makes you cringe on the second listen. Trust the cringe.

## Phase 2 — Scripts 🖥️ (with 📱 thinking time)

You write and curate; AI helps you draft. Your tools: me (Muse), Gemini, or the Gemini Notebook itself — all can turn your sources into spoken-word scripts.

**Adapting vs. reading verbatim — the rule:**
> If a line gives you chills on the page, don't rewrite it. Read it exactly. Bridge *around* it with new narration.

The manifesto was written to be declared; your notebook material was written to be read. The script's job is mostly architecture: cold open → passage → bridge → passage → coda.

**Script formatting for AI voices (this matters):**
- Short sentences. One idea per line — line breaks read as pauses.
- Ellipses for dramatic pauses... but sparingly. They lose power if everywhere.
- Spell out numbers ("four thousand years," not "4000 years").
- Phonetic respelling for anything the casting test flagged: *Enkidu* → "en-KEE-doo" if the voice stumbles.
- Mark emphasis with italics or CAPS for a single word — most TTS engines lean into it slightly.

**Per-episode workflow (repeat × 6):**
1. 📱 Choose the tape's manifesto passage + one notebook thread (the table above is your map).
2. 🖥️ Draft the script — you, me, or Gemini from your sources. Target: ~1,100–1,400 words ≈ 8–10 minutes spoken.
3. 📱 Read it aloud once yourself (privately! car counts) — anything that trips *your* tongue will trip the AI's. Fix it now; regenerating audio costs credits, editing text is free.
4. 🖥️ Finalize the script *before* generating audio. This is the money-saving rule.

**Your `Sovereign manifesto.m4a`:** treat it as a script source, not audio — transcribe the ideas out of it (or just work from the text you wrote). Its job is done; the new voice takes it from here.

## Phase 3 — Audio production 🖥️

**The workflow:** script → AI voice → download → Audacity (voice + music + fades) → MP3 → publish.

**Voice generation — know your budget:**

| Path | Cost | What you get | Trade-off |
|------|------|--------------|-----------|
| **ElevenLabs free** | $0 | 10,000 credits/mo ≈ **10 min audio** | Your chosen voice, full script control. One 8–10 min episode per month, or bank it. |
| **Gemini Notebook Audio Overviews** | $0 | Up to ~30 min per overview, downloadable MP3 | Only 2 fixed voices, no script control (prompt steering only). Good for long episodes free. |
| **ElevenLabs Starter** | $6/mo | 30,000 credits ≈ 30 min, commercial license | The upgrade path when the free tier feels cramped. Not needed to start. |

**Recommended $0 season:** one 8–10 minute tape per month on the ElevenLabs free tier — six months, six tapes, zero dollars. (Or move faster with Gemini Notebook for the longer bridge episodes and save ElevenLabs for the manifesto readings where voice choice matters most.)

**Atmosphere — free music & ambience (verified Oct 2026):**
- **Pixabay Music** (pixabay.com/music) — 290,000+ tracks, free, no attribution required, explicitly allowed in podcasts. Search your vibe: *"dark ambient," "steampunk," "cinematic drone," "clockwork."* One caution: skip tracks with the shield icon (registered with Content ID) — or keep the download receipt.
- **YouTube Audio Library** (studio.youtube.com → Audio Library) — free music *and* a big sound-effects collection (room tone, machinery, atmospheres). Most tracks need no attribution; check each one.
- *Sources: [Pixabay license for podcasts](https://sonilo.com/blog/comparisons/pixabay-music-sound-effects-alternatives-2026) · [podcast music roundup](https://streamyard.com/es-es/blog/free-music-for-podcasts)*

**The sound-design recipe** (keep it simple — atmosphere, not a film score):
1. One ambient bed under the whole episode, low in the mix (~−20 dB under the voice — clearly audible in pauses, felt-not-heard under speech).
2. Fade in over the cold open's first line; fade out under the coda's last line.
3. One sound effect per episode, max — a page turn, a deep bell, static. Restraint is the aesthetic.

**Audacity crash course** (free, desktop — audacityteam.org):
1. Import voice track + music track (File → Import → Audio).
2. Select the music track → Effect → Amplify → −20 dB. That's your bed.
3. Fade in/out: select the edge of a track → Effect → Fade In / Fade Out.
4. Trim mistakes and long silences: select → Delete. Zoom in (Ctrl+scroll) for precision.
5. Select all → Effect → Normalize (evens out volume). Export → MP3, 128 kbps or higher.

**The golden rule of this phase:** finalize the script before you hit generate. Every regeneration spends credits. Text edits are free; audio edits cost.

## Phase 5 — Publishing 🖥️

**Host: Spotify for Creators** (creators.spotify.com) — free, unlimited storage, auto-distributes everywhere via RSS. This is the same host from the brainstorm doc; nothing's changed.

**Setup steps:**
1. Create the account → *New show* → title (*The Manifesto Tapes*), description, upload cover art.
2. Paste the AI-voice disclosure into the show description (below) — do it from episode 1.
3. Upload the pilot MP3 → add episode title + description → publish. You're live on Spotify immediately; Apple Podcasts picks up the RSS feed (submit once via Podcasts Connect with an Apple ID).
4. Per episode after that: upload, describe, publish. That's the whole job.

**🔊 AI-voice disclosure — copy-paste this:**
> *This episode uses AI-generated voices. The research, writing, and direction are human — mine.*

Say a version of it in the audio too (bake it into the coda of Tape One: *"You've been listening to a synthetic voice reading human words."* — honestly, that's a great closing line for the season's theme).

**The rules, short version:** Apple Podcasts requires prominent disclosure of synthetic voices and bans AI impersonation of real people. Spotify removes shows that impersonate another creator's likeness. YouTube requires disclosure on realistic synthetic video. Your hosts are original characters — you're clear. **Never clone a real person's voice** without written permission.

## Companion publication — the evidence locker 📎

The podcast is the spell; the companion is where the receipts live. The tapes make big claims (a tomb under the Euphrates, rocket-fuel rituals, the ledger) — the companion shows the sources, the images, the tools. It also gives the season its second frequency in written form.

- **Platform: Substack.** Free, built for exactly this (newsletter + podcast companion in one place, audio embeds, subscriber model ready when there's an audience). Not Patreon — that's for monetizing an audience you don't have yet. Not Medium — wrong fit, declining for this kind of work.
- **The elegant shape:** every Field Note doubles as a newsletter issue. The interstitial gives the listener the tool; the post gives them the links, the screenshots, the step-by-step. Tape posts carry transcripts, source lists, and images (cuneiform tablets, Derinkuyu maps, Parsons' lab notes).
- **Cadence:** one post per tape + one per field note. Write the post from the episode script — don't create new work, reframe it.
- **Name it like the show's shadow:** something like *The Ledger* or *Field Notes from the Manifesto Tapes*. Decide during the pilot weekend.
- **Phase 0 (free, 📱):** create the Substack, write the "what is this" welcome post, publish the pilot tape's transcript + sources as issue #1. That alone is a real artifact even before the season exists.

## Style notes — Moonrise (Washington Post, Lillian Cunningham)

*Ash flagged this show as compelling, 2026-10-10. Notes on what to steal — filed here so the production plan carries them.*

**The show:** *Moonrise* — "Truer, but also darker." The real origin story behind America's moonshot: nuclear brinkmanship, backroom politics, sci-fi influence, the von Braun chapter ("a German rocket scientist with a secret mission and dark past"). Note the overlap: Moonrise's territory *is* her FN 3.5 territory. Her show goes further — and stranger.

**What to take:**

1. **"Truer, but also darker" as a positioning sentence.** Every episode punctures the official story with the wilder real one. The Manifesto Tapes does the same move ("the system isn't broken — it's functioning as designed"). Consider a one-line version for the show's own positioning.
2. **Retro texture as identity.** Moonrise's producer built a distinct "retro sci-fi feel" for the whole show — that's exactly what the found-tape layer does here. Commit to it as *the* sound, not decoration.
3. **"Beginnings can manipulate, endings can manipulate."** (Cunningham on bookending.) The Tape 1 cold open and the Tape 6 silence are already doing this — treat them as load-bearing, not nice-to-haves.
4. **Chapter framing.** Moonrise calls episodes "chapters" — literary, deliberate. Her "Tapes" naming does the same work. Keep it.
5. **Episode descriptions as hooks.** Moonrise's loglines are short, present-tense, evocative ("A man who meets himself on the moon. A blue orb that bursts into flames."). Use this style for the Substack issue headers and podcast descriptions — not summaries, *lures*.
6. **Two-person team.** Moonrise was essentially Lillian + one producer. Ash + Wren is the same shape. Small teams make distinctive shows.
7. **Give the erased their due.** Moonrise devoted real estate to the forgotten Soviet program (Korolev). Her "provenance reading" tool (FN 3.5) is the same instinct — say it as a principle: *the show finds the erased name.*
