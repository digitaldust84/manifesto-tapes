# Field Notes 1.5 — The Local Stack
*Assembly 25% — the heist kit*

*Draft script, ~7 minutes. Two hosts: SABLE (cool, dry, full archivist mode) and COG (earnest).*

---

*[Cold open. Dark ambient bed, low. Then a key turning in a lock — close-mic'd, the tumblers clicking one by one. It holds, then the drone drops out.]*

**COG:** That's a key.

**SABLE:** A brass one. It opens something. That's increasingly rare.

**COG:** What does it open?

**SABLE:** Doesn't matter. What matters is that *I* hold it. Nobody rents it to me. Nobody can revoke it with a software update. When I turn it, the door opens — no terms of service.

**COG:** Okay... why are we talking about keys?

**SABLE:** Because every time you tap "Sign in with Google," you're turning a key someone else owns, in a lock someone else built, on a door to a room where they write down everything you do.

**COG:** It's just a login, Sable. It's convenient.

**SABLE:** *(dry)* "Convenient" is what the ledger has always called itself. The Akkadian farmer thought the tablet was convenient too. Then the temple knew exactly how much barley he owed — forever.

**COG:** So what's the alternative? I can't just... not log in to things.

**SABLE:** You can log in differently. That's what this is. Field Notes 1.5: the first tool in the kit. Assembly twenty-five percent.

*[A soft click — like a switchboard plug seating. Under it, faintly: 1920s telephone operators, patch-cable clicks, murmured numbers. The Machine Ledger, Tier 2.]*

**SABLE:** Before the cloud, there was a woman with a patch cable. She connected your call by hand — and she worked for the phone company. Your voice traveled through her hands. The network was never yours, Cog. It just got better at hiding that.

**COG:** That's bleak.

**SABLE:** That's the diagnosis. Here's the tool. Three moves. First: kill the single sign-on.

**COG:** But it's so *easy*. One button—

**SABLE:** That's the snare. "Sign in with Google" means one company holds the master key to everywhere you've ever signed in. One ledger, your whole life in the column.

**COG:** It's just words on a button, though.

**SABLE:** *(dry)* "Person." "Understand." "Agree." You clicked words you never defined, in contracts you never read, binding a self you never got to describe. That's not convenience, Cog — that's the oldest snare there is: the ledger doesn't just record you, it *names* you first.

**COG:** ...Okay. That's actually chilling.

**SABLE:** Good. So: a password manager. One master password that lives in *your* head, unique keys for every door. An afternoon to set up. The receipts — which manager, how to migrate — are in the locker.

**COG:** One afternoon. Okay. What's two?

**SABLE:** Run a mind that lives in your house.

**COG:** ...A what?

**SABLE:** A local language model. Free, runs on your own machine, nothing leaves the room. You ask it things, it answers, and no company writes down what you asked. The cloud model is a scribe who reports to the temple. The local model is a scribe who works for *you*.

**COG:** Does it work as well?

**SABLE:** For most things you actually need? Yes. And the part it does best is the part that matters: it tells no one. Two sentences, Cog — the setup guides are in the locker.

**SABLE:** Third: lock down what's already out there. Download your data from the big accounts. Delete what you don't need. Turn off the ad personalization, the location history, the "helpful" timelines. You're not erasing the ledger — you're just... writing smaller in it.

**COG:** This all sounds like a lot.

**SABLE:** It's three afternoons, spread across a month. And here's the part the ledger doesn't want you to know: *it compounds.* Every account you move off single sign-on, every question you ask the local mind instead of the cloud — that's one fewer line in their book. The refusal from Tape 1, made concrete.

**COG:** The refusal had a to-do list all along.

**SABLE:** *(the faintest warmth)* It did. — The receipts are in the locker. Guides, download links, the setup walkthroughs — all of it.

*[The switchboard clicks fade. One last sound: the brass key turning again, slow.]*

**SABLE:** The ledger is clay. Carve your own line.

*[Cut to silence.]*

### Production notes
- **Object:** the brass key — opened on, the episode's thesis in metal. Tumblers close-mic'd.
- **Found-tape texture:** 1920s switchboard operators (Chinatown Telephone Operators, CC0) under the network passage — the original network: human hands, company-owned. Tier 2 treatment per the sound design doc.
- **Sable's arc beat:** full archivist mode — cool, dry, academic. Pre-crack. The wit lands cleanly here; contrast with 2.5.
- **Heist kit:** Assembly 25%. Tool handed to listener: **the key** — password manager (kill SSO) + one local model + data lockdown. The refusal made concrete.
- **Two-sentence rule honored throughout** — setup depth lives in the locker (local-LLM guides, password-manager migration).
- **Word count:** ~950 words of dialogue ≈ 6.5–7 minutes audio.
