# Field Notes 3.5 — The Alchemist of Pasadena
*Assembly 75% — the heist kit*

*Draft script, ~7 minutes. Two hosts: SABLE (dry, skeptical, resisting) and COG (earnest, steady).*

---

*[Cold open. Dark ambient bed, low. Then test-bench static rises — a whining build — and under it, flattened, ghostly Mission Control chatter. The "ritual rocket" soundscape. It holds for five seconds, then cuts hard to silence.]*

**COG:** That's not a launch.

**SABLE:** *(dry)* No. That's a test stand. Somewhere in the desert, a tube of fuel is about to become a god for nine seconds.

*[A metallic clunk — close-mic'd, intimate.]*

**SABLE:** This is a solid-fuel casing. Steel tube, about so long. The fuel that goes inside it was invented by a man named Jack Parsons, in Pasadena, in the 1940s. He also tried to summon the goddess Babalon in the desert with L. Ron Hubbard, using rituals John Dee wrote for Queen Elizabeth four hundred years earlier.

**COG:** Wait. The rocket guy and the ritual guy are the *same* guy?

**SABLE:** The same guy. That's the problem. That's why we're here.

**COG:** Okay, but — how does a rocket scientist end up doing desert rituals?

**SABLE:** Because in 1940, rocket science *was* a desert ritual. There was no NASA. There was a group of men in Pasadena — Parsons, von Kármán's students — calling themselves the Suicide Squad, blowing things up in the Arroyo Seco because no university would touch them. Parsons gave them the fuel that worked: castable solid propellant. The JATO units. That's what got American rockets off the ground — literally. Strap them to a plane, light the tube, you're airborne.

**COG:** So he's a hero of the space age.

**SABLE:** He co-founded the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Then the FBI opened a file on him an inch thick, revoked his security clearance in 1952, and he died that same year in an explosion in his own garage laboratory. He was thirty-seven.

*[Beat. The static breathes underneath, low.]*

**COG:** The rituals, though. Were they — real? Like, did he actually—

**SABLE:** *(sharper than usual)* Cog. This is where archives get people killed.

**COG:** I'm serious. Did it work?

**SABLE:** *(a pause — the deflection doesn't come)* He believed it worked. In 1946 he performed the Babalon Working — Enochian calls, sex magic, a desert working meant to incarnate a goddess. His partner in it later founded Scientology. Draw whatever lines you want; I'm just the archivist.

**COG:** What did he think would happen? The goddess — was she supposed to just... show up?

**SABLE:** He called it the Babalon Working. Enochian calls, ritual sex magic in the desert, meant to incarnate a divine feminine force into the world. *(beat)* Whatever you think of the method, Cog — look at the ambition. He wasn't trying to build a better bomb. He was trying to change what a human being *is*.

**COG:** You're deflecting.

**SABLE:** I'm *filing*. There's a difference.

**COG:** Fine. File this: if the rituals were nonsense, why did the state keep doing them?

*[Silence. The drone drops out entirely.]*

**SABLE:** ...Say that again.

**COG:** They pushed Parsons out. Took his clearance, buried his name — JPL barely said it out loud for decades. But the *work* didn't stop. The files say so. Declassified CIA documents — whole programs, the seventies through the nineties — remote viewing, consciousness research, men in rooms trying to see across the world with their minds. STARGATE. That's twenty years *after* Parsons died.

**COG:** With their minds. Officially. With budgets.

**SABLE:** With budgets, oversight committees, and twenty years of paperwork. Declassified in '95. You can read the files yourself.

**SABLE:** *(quiet)* They fired the magician and kept the magic.

**COG:** So who replaced him? If Parsons was too strange to keep — who was strange in the *right* way?

**SABLE:** His name was Wernher von Braun. He built the V-2 for the Third Reich — the rocket that fell on London. After the war, Operation Paperclip brought him and a hundred-odd German engineers to America, scrubbed their files, and handed them the space program. Von Braun went on to run NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center. Built the Saturn V. The moon rocket.

**COG:** The occultist gets erased. The Nazi engineer gets a parade.

**COG:** How do you scrub a man who built rockets for the Reich?

**SABLE:** You stamp the file. *(beat)* Paper covers everything, Cog. That's what it's for.

**SABLE:** Parsons summoned. Von Braun signed the paperwork. That's the whole story of the twentieth century in one sentence, Cog. The wild origin gets filed, numbered, and controlled. The ritual becomes the bureaucracy.

**COG:** ...That's the ledger again, isn't it.

**SABLE:** *(beat)* Don't.

**COG:** It is, though. Clay tablet, paper file — same book. Parsons wouldn't sign the right papers, so they wrote him *out* of the papers.

**SABLE:** *(long pause — when she speaks again, the dryness is gone)* If they use rituals to bind each other — the oaths, the clearances, the compartments — what binds *us*?

**COG:** I was going to ask you that.

**SABLE:** The file, Cog. The file binds us. Your name in the database, your face in the system, your debt in the column. The ritual and the bureaucracy were never opposites. That was the lie. The binding is the same — only the robes changed.

*[The static returns, softer now. Under it, faintly, the wax-cylinder crackle — the oldest voice on the ledger, waiting three tapes away.]*

**COG:** So what's the tool? Field Notes are supposed to hand me something.

**SABLE:** The tool is provenance. Pick one technology you touch every day — your phone, your map, your medicine. Find the name they erased from its history. Read the file they didn't want kept. The wild origin is always in the footnotes, Cog. That's how you read the signs: *look for who got written out.*

**COG:** That's... actually doable.

**SABLE:** Start with this one. Parsons co-founded JPL, and for decades his name wasn't on the building. Find the erased name — then ask what else got erased with him.

**SABLE:** The best tools are. — The receipts are in the locker. The STARGATE files, the Paperclip manifests, Parsons' FBI file — all of it, linked and cited.

*[The drone fades to almost nothing. One last sound: paper — a page turning, close-mic'd.]*

**SABLE:** *(barely above a whisper)* The ledger is paper. For now.

*[Cut to silence.]*

### Production notes
- **Object:** the solid-fuel casing — opened on, never explained twice. Steel, close-mic'd clunk.
- **Found-tape texture:** "ritual rocket" soundscape (Apollo 15 Mission Control chatter + test-bench static) under the cold open and the STARGATE passage; wax-cylinder crackle as a ghost under the closing (foreshadows the finale).
- **Sable's arc beat:** this is her resistance episode. She deflects twice ("I'm filing"), goes quiet once, and finally answers Cog's question honestly — but she does *not* convert. The witness turn waits for Tape 4.
- **Heist kit:** Assembly 75%. Tool handed to listener: **provenance reading** — find the erased name in any technology's history.
- **Two-sentence rule honored throughout** — depth lives in the locker.
- **Word count:** ~920 words of dialogue ≈ 6.5 minutes audio.
