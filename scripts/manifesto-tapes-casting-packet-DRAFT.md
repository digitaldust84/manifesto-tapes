## The two reads

- **Sable Vane** — dry, archivist, female-presenting. Not a villain, not a whisper-ASMR cliché. She reads like someone who has catalogued terrible things and refuses to flinch: flat affect with buried feeling.
- **Cog** — earnest, naive, lighter register. Curious, warm, a little fast. Not cartoonish, not a robot voice — a younger believer who asks the questions Sable won't.

## Audition lines (from the locked "Tape Zero" pilot script)

Run every candidate voice through the SAME three tests so you're comparing apples to apples:

**Test 1 — Sable's authority** (the thesis read):
> "You feel tired in a way sleep doesn't fix. That's not a feeling. That's a *reading*. Your nervous system is doing exactly what it was tuned to do — producing data."

**Test 2 — Cog's warmth** (the cold open):
> "It's 2:14 in the morning. You're still awake. I'm not judging — I'm a construct, I don't sleep."

**Test 3 — Chemistry** (the intro exchange; generate both voices, play back-to-back):
> Sable: "I'm Sable Vane. I archive things that were never supposed to survive."
> Cog: "And I'm Cog. I ask the questions she won't."

## What to listen for

- **Clarity under the bed.** The music sits at −20 dB, so voices must stay intelligible without you straining. If you have to concentrate to catch words, reject the voice.
- **Sable's dryness.** She should sound like she's reading a file, not performing. Beware voices that turn "dry" into "bored" — you want controlled, not flatlined.
- **Cog's earnestness.** Beware voices that turn "earnest" into "salesy" or "chipmunk." You want a smart friend at 2am, not a mascot.
- **The tape test.** Per the Moonrise craft notes, retro found-tape texture is the show's identity. The voices should feel like they belong on worn tape — a little grit is fine, even good.
- **The chills preview.** After narrowing to two finalists per host, run Test 1 + Test 3 and listen in the parked car — your real test environment. The pilot's go/no-go is chills; the casting should aim at the same bar.

## Music + SFX playbook (Pixabay, all free)

- Go to pixabay.com/music — royalty-free, no attribution required, commercial use OK.
- Search: **"dark ambient"**, **"cinematic underscore"**, **"tension"**.
- Shortlist 2–3 candidates, each 2–3 minutes. Rules: no hooks or melodies (they fight the narration), no vocals, no horror-movie stingers (too aggressive for this show), nothing bright or upbeat.
- Mix the bed at **−20 dB** under the voices in Audacity (per the production plan).
- Phone-notification SFX for the cold open: search Pixabay sound effects for "phone notification" — close and intimate, not a default phone ping everyone recognizes.
