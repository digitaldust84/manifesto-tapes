# Field Notes 2.5 — The Safe Mode
*Assembly 50% — the heist kit*

*Draft script, ~7 minutes. Two hosts: SABLE (dry, armor cracking) and COG (earnest, steadier than before).*

---

*[Cold open. Low drone. Then stone grinding on stone — slow, massive, close. It goes on a beat too long, then stops. Silence.]*

**COG:** What *is* that?

**SABLE:** A door. Half a ton of it, rolling in a carved channel.

**COG:** A door that heavy—

**SABLE:** In Derinkuyu, in Cappadocia — a city carved eighteen levels down into the rock, room for twenty thousand people — the doors were single slabs of stone. And they only closed from the *inside*.

**COG:** ...That's the most reassuring thing you've ever said.

**SABLE:** *(dry, but thinner than usual)* Don't get used to it.

*[Under the drone: a cheerful 1950s jingle — "Bert the Turtle was very alert" — Duck and Cover, Tier 2, slightly too bright against the dark.]*

**COG:** Is that—

**SABLE:** Bert the Turtle. 1951. Duck and cover, children. The state taught an entire generation to hide under their desks from the end of the world — and called it preparedness.

**COG:** That's not preparedness, that's—

**SABLE:** Theater. Panic with a lesson plan. Now listen to this instead. February 1942. Los Angeles. Air-raid sirens, blackout, searchlights — the army fires *fourteen hundred* anti-aircraft shells into the night sky.

**COG:** At what?

**SABLE:** Nothing. Weather balloons, nerves, a war on. Five people died — from the shells, from heart attacks, from cars crashing in the blackout. Not one enemy aircraft. Ever.

**COG:** Fourteen hundred shells at an empty sky.

**SABLE:** That's what panic does, Cog. It spends everything and hits nothing. The Safe Mode is the opposite of that night.

*[The jingle cuts. The drone returns, lower.]*

**COG:** Okay, so — the dread is real, though. Right? We're not pretending the world isn't... like this.

**SABLE:** No. We're not pretending. Authoritarian drift, supply shocks, the whole menu — it's real, and your nervous system knows it. That's Tape 2. I'm not here to talk you out of the feeling.

**COG:** Then what are we doing?

**SABLE:** *(a pause — quieter)* ...Some things aren't filed, Cog. They're felt. I keep paper maps in my desk. I checked the locks twice last night. The archivist gets scared too. The difference is what you *do* with it.

**COG:** Which is?

**SABLE:** Derinkuyu. That city wasn't built in a panic — it was carved over *generations*. Tunnels, wells, chapels, schools, eighteen levels down. When the armies came, twenty thousand people walked underground and *waited* — calm, supplied, organized. No fourteen hundred shells. No empty sky. Just a door that closed from the inside.

**SABLE:** So here's the protocol. Three layers. First: analog redundancies. Paper copies of what matters — documents, contacts, maps. Cash, somewhere sensible. A radio that doesn't need the grid. The cloud is rented memory; paper is yours.

**COG:** A radio? Like—

**SABLE:** Like your grandparents had. It works when nothing else does, and nobody logs what you hear. The lists are in the locker.

**SABLE:** Second: the local mesh. Know your neighbors' names. Know who's a nurse, who has tools, who has a generator. Mesh networks — phones talking to phones with no tower — the guides are in the locker. The most resilient network ever built is a street that trusts itself.

**COG:** That's... not what I expected. I expected bunkers and beans.

**SABLE:** Beans are fine. But bunkers are a *mood*, Cog, not a plan. Third layer — and this is the one nobody sells you: mental armor.

*[Under the drone, faintly: a flat 1960s narrator — "this is what America would look like" — RED NIGHTMARE, Tier 2, calm as a weather report.]*

**SABLE:** Listen to that voice. No urgency, no shouting — just dread, administered. The panic cycle is manufactured, Cog. The feed needs you afraid by the hour; fear is the highest-engagement emotion ever measured.

*[The narration cuts.]*

**SABLE:** So you ration it. Check the news twice a day, like meals. The rest of the time — *live* in the part of the world you can touch.

**COG:** The news is weather, not a window.

**SABLE:** *(beat — the faintest approval)* ...Yes. Exactly that.

**SABLE:** Panic spends fourteen hundred shells at an empty sky. The Safe Mode rolls one stone door shut — from the inside — and waits, supplied, with people you trust. That's the whole protocol. — The receipts are in the locker. Derinkuyu's plans, the mesh guides, the radio lists.

*[The drone thins. One last sound: stone grinding, slow — then the soft finality of it seating.]*

**SABLE:** *(barely above a whisper)* The ledger is stone. The door closes from the inside.

*[Cut to silence.]*

---

### Production notes
- **Object:** the rolling stone door — Derinkuyu's half-ton slab. Opened on, the episode's thesis in stone: safety you control.
- **Found-tape texture:** *Duck and Cover* (1951) Bert jingle, ironic, under the panic passage; *RED NIGHTMARE* flat narration available for the dread passage (use sparingly). Tier 2 treatment.
- **Sable's arc beat:** the crack widens — post-Tape-2. She admits fear ("I checked the locks twice last night") and speaks the "felt, not filed" line. Still dry, but audibly thinner than 1.5. No jokes in the back half.
- **Heist kit:** Assembly 50%. Tool handed to listener: **the Safe Mode protocol** — analog redundancies, local mesh, mental armor (panic-cycle rationing).
- **Two-sentence rule honored** — mesh/radio guides live in the locker.
- **Word count:** ~980 words of dialogue ≈ 7 minutes audio.
